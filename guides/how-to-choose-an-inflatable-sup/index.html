How to choose an inflatable SUP board

The right stand up paddle board starts with a simple question: what would a good day on the water look like for you?

Perhaps it’s a quiet lap of the lake, a gentle stretch on a sheltered bay, or a longer paddle with somewhere new to explore. Choose for the trips you’ll actually take, then check the board’s dimensions, carrying capacity and setup. Here’s how to narrow it down.

01 · Use

1. Start with your usual paddle

  • Relaxed cruising: an all-round shape is a useful starting point for learning, turning and short recreational outings.
  • Yoga and gentle movement: prioritise deck space, width and a layout that gives you room to change position.
  • Fishing: check usable deck space, gear attachment points and the load you’ll carry, including tackle and water.
  • Longer routes and fitness: a longer, more streamlined board can suit paddlers who value glide and tracking, meaning how well it holds a straight course.

Think about your normal launch spot too. A sheltered lake and an exposed coastal route call for different skills and preparation. A touring label doesn’t make a board, or its rider, ready for every condition.

02 · Dimensions

2. Read the dimensions together

Length: longer boards generally favour glide over distance. Shorter all-round boards can be easier to manoeuvre and manage on land.

Width: extra width usually gives you more room and initial stability. A narrower board can suit distance-focused paddling, but may feel less forgiving while you learn. Consider whether you can reach comfortably over the side for a paddle stroke.

Thickness and volume: these contribute to the overall design and buoyancy, but neither tells you everything about how a board will feel. Shape, construction, inflation pressure and your load all matter.

If possible, take a lesson or try a similar-sized board before choosing. A useful size is one you can paddle, turn, carry and climb back onto comfortably.

03 · Width

3. When a wide inflatable paddle board helps

A wider board generally offers more initial stability and more room to move, but the best width still depends on your reach, total load and intended use. You need to reach the water comfortably for an effective paddle stroke, carry the board to the launch and climb back onto it.

AURA’s Yoga Cruiser is 36 inches wide — 91.44 cm, or about 91 cm — and 11 feet long. Its broad padded deck is intended for yoga, gentle movement and relaxed cruising. The CoastGo is 33 inches wide and 10'6" long, a more compact all-round format for straightforward recreational paddling.

Choose the 36-inch Yoga Cruiser when

Yoga, changing position and generous usable deck space matter more than covering distance efficiently.

Choose the 33-inch CoastGo when

You want an everyday all-round outline and a simpler hand-pump package rather than the widest deck in the range.

Width does not work alone. Yoga Cruiser lists an 180 kg maximum load, a wheeled bag and an electric pump that needs a compatible car power socket. CoastGo lists a 120 kg maximum load, a carry bag and a hand pump. Compare the whole setup with your normal launch and storage route.

04 · Shared outings

4. Choose for family outings, not for a headcount

A family outing can mean different things. Decide which of these you are planning before comparing boards:

  • One board shared in turns: choose a size and setup that every intended paddler can handle. Check each person’s total load, reach and experience, not only the smallest paddler.
  • Several people paddling at the same time: plan a suitable board and safety equipment for each paddler.
  • A passenger on the same board: ask for model-specific guidance before ordering. Spare capacity does not establish that a board is designed or approved for tandem use.

For relaxed turn-taking, compare how quickly the group can set up and pack down. CoastGo’s hand pump works without vehicle power. Yoga Cruiser’s electric pump reduces manual inflation effort but needs a compatible car power socket, and its wheeled bag is most useful where the route suits wheels.

Maximum capacity is still only a load limit. Add the paddler, clothing and everything carried. It is not a recommended rider weight, a child-suitability claim or permission for more than one person to ride.

05 · Load

5. Count everything the board will carry

Add your weight, clothing and all equipment. Include drinking water, a dry bag and any other gear.

Maximum capacity is a load limit, not a recommended rider weight or a promise of comfortable performance at that limit. Stay within the stated maximum, and ask for sizing advice if your total load is close to it. Experience, balance and conditions affect how much room for comfort you need.

For example, an 85 kg paddler carrying 10 kg of equipment has a 95 kg load. That calculation helps you compare boards; it doesn’t establish which one will feel best. Nor does spare capacity automatically make a board suitable for an extra person.

07 · Setup

7. Check the whole package

All four packages list an adjustable three-piece paddle, bag, leash, removable fins, waterproof phone pouch and repair kit. CoastGo includes a hand pump and carry bag. The other three include an electric pump and wheeled bag.

The electric pump needs a compatible car power socket. Plan where you’ll inflate, check the pump’s connection requirements, and consider the distance from your vehicle to the water. A hand pump works without vehicle power, but adds physical effort.

Check packed dimensions and total package weight against your boot, storage space and carrying route. Wheels help on suitable surfaces; sand, steps and uneven paths may still mean carrying everything.

Before each outing, inflate to the pressure specified for your exact board and follow its manual. Never exceed its limit or copy a PSI setting from another model. Follow the manufacturer’s heat, cleaning, drying and storage instructions too.

08 · Safety

8. Budget for safe paddling

Allow for a properly fitted, activity-appropriate lifejacket or PFD, sun protection and clothing suited to water temperature. Wear your lifejacket and check the rules for your state and waterway. NSW paddle safety guidance also recommends checking conditions, telling someone your plan and paddling with company where possible.

A supplied leash isn’t suitable for every environment. Avoid weirs and moving-water hazards as a beginner.

Check wind, weather, tides and local hazards before launching. Avoid offshore winds, even when the surface looks calm. Keep a suitable means of calling for help on your person, and learn how to get back onto your board.

09 · FAQ

Common questions

What does iSUP mean?

iSUP means inflatable stand up paddle board. It can be deflated for transport and storage, then inflated before paddling.

Is a wider board always better for beginners?

Width can help with initial stability, but fit also depends on your size, total load, reach and intended use. Compare the whole design.

Can I use a yoga board for ordinary paddling?

Yes. AURA’s Yoga Cruiser is also designed for relaxed cruising. Choose it for deck space and easy-paced outings; compare a touring shape if covering distance is your main goal.

Does a complete package include everything I need?

Check the actual list. AURA’s listed packages don’t include a lifejacket, and your location may require additional safety equipment.
